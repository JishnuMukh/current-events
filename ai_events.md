## Google Introduces WeatherNext 3 for More Frequent, Finer-Grained Forecasts

**Published: September 3, 2026**

Google DeepMind and Google Research announced **WeatherNext 3**, a weather-forecasting model that incorporates live geostationary-satellite observations. Google says the system can issue global forecasts every hour and represent some surface conditions at up to 5-kilometer resolution. The company is integrating the model's outputs into products including Search, Gemini, Maps, Google Maps Platform, and Google Cloud.

**Why it matters:** Weather affects decisions far beyond daily planning, including disaster preparation, farming, power generation, and supply chains. A model that can refresh from current satellite observations and deliver more local detail could make AI forecasting more useful where fast-changing conditions, especially precipitation, are important.

## California Enacts New Safeguards for Children, AI Chatbots, and Online Feeds

**Event date: September 10, 2026**

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed a package of technology-safety laws focused on children. According to the Associated Press, the measures include requirements for AI-chatbot operators to conduct risk assessments before deployment, restrictions on addictive feeds for people under 16, and potential penalties for large social-media companies found negligent in harming children. The package also includes an option for families to decline school-issued laptops.

**Why it matters:** California is a major center of technology development, so its rules can shape product design and influence policy discussions elsewhere. The legislation moves the debate beyond voluntary promises by putting obligations around youth safety and chatbot risk assessment into state law.

## Anthropic Says It Blocked AI Misuse Tied to Cyber, Surveillance, and Biological Risks

**Published: September 10, 2026**

Anthropic said it had stopped actors from attempting to use its AI systems for malicious activity, including cyberattacks, surveillance, and research that could contribute to biological weapons. AP reported that the company's third misuse report described activity observed between December 2025 and August 2026, including an attempt to obtain help with a funding proposal involving changes to the chikungunya virus. Anthropic said it had strengthened safeguards in newer models to limit assistance with dual-use biological research.

**Why it matters:** The report illustrates how general-purpose AI can lower barriers for harmful work even when a model is not itself carrying out an attack or experiment. It also highlights the growing importance of monitoring, access controls, and transparent reporting as AI providers deploy more capable tools.

## OpenAI Calls for Shared AI Safety Rules and Backs California Bills

**Published: September 9, 2026**

OpenAI published a policy statement calling for mandatory, capability-based U.S. AI-safety requirements and compatible international approaches to measuring capabilities and managing risk. The company also said it supported four California proposals: SB 813 on infrastructure for independent safety assessments, AB 1405 on AI-auditor standards, SB 1119 on protections for young people, and AB 1864 on AI-enabled biological-threat safeguards. The statement frames these positions as OpenAI's policy advocacy, not as enacted federal policy.

**Why it matters:** The announcement shows that arguments over frontier-AI governance now include concrete proposals for independent evaluation, auditing, youth protections, and biological-risk safeguards. Whether governments adopt such approaches will help determine how quickly voluntary company practices become enforceable standards.
